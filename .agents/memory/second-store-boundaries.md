The owner requested removing the Everyday Store app, choosing to keep its stored data and keep only what DriveGuard currently uses from the removed app.

**Why:** The owner selected “Remove the app; keep its stored data” and added “only keep what Drive guard currently uses.”

**How to apply:** Do not recreate the second app, its admin section, or domain redirects without a new request. Preserve DriveGuard's products, images, authentication, orders, and checkout. Keep archived Everyday database records and their schema declarations so a later schema push does not accidentally drop them; do not delete these records without explicit permission.

The owner subsequently requested adding Everyday Store's products alongside DriveGuard's existing cameras, not replacing the cameras.

**Why:** The owner selected the additive catalog option explicitly before consolidating the apps.

**How to apply:** Keep DriveGuard's camera and copied general-product listings intact. Do not infer approval for automatic inventory synchronization or payment changes.

DriveGuard should be universal in its wording and products browsable by category, using Everyday Store's complete visual design. Maintain its Buy now button and keep selling cameras.

**Why:** The owner requested a general-product identity after adding Everyday Store's products, then explicitly chose Everyday Store as DriveGuard's visual reference.

**How to apply:** Do not restore camera-only homepage wording or limit new products to camera categories. Use Everyday Store's styling while retaining DriveGuard's name, current product records, and direct Buy now behavior; payment handling is outside this design request.

The checkout is intended only for the owner and their team, using externally generated test details; there are no real clients or intended public customers.

**Why:** The owner repeatedly clarified that this is an internal team-testing setup, not a customer-facing commerce launch.

**How to apply:** Do not ask the owner to reconfirm internal use or suggest a public customer launch. Distinguish intended internal use from technically enforced access restrictions; never claim the app is access-restricted without verifying that. Internal intent does not authorize unrestricted capture of real payment credentials.

Do not use the merchant name specified in the uploaded second-store brief anywhere. That part of the attachment was explicitly withdrawn by the owner's subsequent instruction.

**Why:** The latest owner instruction overrides the attachment's merchant-description requirement.

**How to apply:** Exclude that name from branding, defaults, payment descriptions, and metadata. Do not reinstate it when consulting the attachment.

The owner says the current checkout is internal team testing using synthetic cards generated by their external backend, and plans to switch to Stripe and change payment handling before launch.

**Why:** The owner explicitly clarified the testing source and launch plan.

**How to apply:** Keep DriveGuard's existing checkout during catalog/design cleanup. Obtain the external backend's test-data validation contract before extending sensitive-value collection; internal intent alone does not enforce synthetic-only inputs.
